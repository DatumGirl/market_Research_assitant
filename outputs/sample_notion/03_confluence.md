# Competitor Briefing: Confluence
_Prepared for: Notion · Generated: 2026-08-30 · Sources: 27_

## Snapshot
Confluence is Atlassian's team workspace and knowledge-management platform for creating, organizing, and collaborating on documentation, wikis, and project content. It serves teams from 10-person startups up to enterprises with 150,000+ users, spanning IT/software, product management, marketing, HR, and cross-functional knowledge sharing, with especially deep adoption among teams already using Jira and other Atlassian tools. Enlyft data puts Confluence's software market share at roughly 0.4%, with heaviest use in IT services, computer software, and financial services, and Atlassian tracks data on 169,261 companies using the product.

## Core Features
- **Unlimited pages and spaces (even on Free)** — Lowers the barrier for small teams to adopt without hitting content caps, competing directly with Notion's free/entry tiers on raw capacity. [^5]
- **Deep native integration with Jira, Bitbucket, and Trello** — Creates workflow continuity and traceability between docs and dev tickets that a standalone workspace tool can't replicate without custom integration work, driving lock-in for engineering-heavy orgs. [^12]
- **Whiteboards (up to 3 active per user on Free)** — Adds visual brainstorming/collaboration alongside docs, encroaching on territory Notion and Miro-adjacent tools also compete for. [^7]
- **Smart Links / third-party integrations (1,000+ products)** — Reduces context-switching by embedding external tools (Figma, Miro, Google Drive, Canva, Loom) directly in pages, matching a key Notion selling point. [^11]
- **Version history and page comparison/revert** — Table-stakes for enterprise documentation trust; important for teams evaluating migration risk from either platform. [^10]
- **Enterprise admin/security controls (SAML SSO, audit logs, data residency, automation)** — Signals Confluence's push upmarket into regulated, large-enterprise accounts where Notion has historically been weaker on compliance depth. [^7]
- **Rovo AI features (paid tiers)** — Atlassian's AI layer competes with Notion AI for the emerging 'AI-native workspace' positioning that both companies are racing toward. [^5]

## Market Positioning
- **Segment:** Team collaboration and knowledge-management/wiki software, spanning small teams to large enterprises
- **Ideal customer:** Organizations already invested in the Atlassian ecosystem (especially Jira users) needing centralized documentation tied to development and project workflows — IT/software, product management, marketing, HR, and cross-functional teams; skews toward mid-sized to large enterprises with thousands of employees
- **Differentiation:** Seamless connectivity with Atlassian's broader product suite (Jira, Trello, Bitbucket), creating workflow continuity and documentation-to-ticket traceability that standalone tools reportedly can't match, plus ecosystem lock-in benefits for multi-product Atlassian customers
- **Go-to-market:** Primarily self-serve/product-led for Free and Standard tiers with online sign-up, supplemented by direct sales for enterprise deployments (Data Center/enterprise tier); marketplace app ecosystem for extensibility

## Strategic Implications for Notion
- **Threat:** Deep native Jira/Bitbucket/Trello integration creates strong lock-in for engineering-heavy organizations that Notion cannot easily replicate, making displacement harder in dev-centric accounts.
- **Threat:** Free tier now includes unlimited pages/spaces and whiteboards for up to 10 users, narrowing the functional gap with Notion's free/team tiers and reducing a key reason for small teams to switch.
- **Threat:** Atlassian's Rovo AI rollout across paid tiers directly contests Notion AI as the two vendors converge on 'AI-native workspace' positioning.
- **Threat:** Confluence's enterprise security/admin stack (SAML SSO, data residency, audit logs) and broad customer base (169,261 companies tracked) signal continued strength in regulated, large-enterprise accounts where Notion has had to build up credibility.
- **Opportunity:** Multiple independent sources flag rising, inconsistent, and hard-to-predict per-user costs at scale (compounding app/marketplace fees) — Notion can market simpler, more predictable pricing to cost-sensitive growing teams.
- **Opportunity:** Reviews note Confluence's feature set can feel like 'overkill' for small, simple projects, suggesting an opening for Notion to win lightweight-use-case teams that don't need the full Atlassian ecosystem.
- **Opportunity:** Confluence's low overall software market share (~0.4% per Enlyft) suggests the knowledge-management category remains fragmented and winnable rather than dominated by a single incumbent.
- **Opportunity:** Support for paid tiers is community-based unless on higher plans, a potential service-quality gap Notion could highlight in competitive comparisons.
